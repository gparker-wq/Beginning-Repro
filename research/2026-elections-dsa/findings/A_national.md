**Why the numbers differ.**
- The called-seat floors (228, 229, 231, 242) depend on when each outlet took its snapshot. They also depend on whether the outlet uses AP calls (TEGNA, Ballotpedia) or its own decision desk (NBC, CBS, CNBC).
- "Net gain" depends on whether the outlet counts only races called so far (+18, +25) or its projection (+35 or more).
- The baselines also differ. CBS says Democrats held 214 seats going in. Some outlets count vacancies differently.
- All sources agree that Democrats have passed the 218 needed for a majority.
- **Best defensible range as of midday Nov 4:** about 245–268 D seats. A ~258 point estimate (NBC) would mean a net gain of roughly +40 to +44 from a 214–218 starting base. The final count is not determined.

### 6.2 Is 2026 a "wave"?
- **Senate:** A net loss of 7 or more seats for the president's party is the largest since at least 1980 (when Republicans gained 12). It is roughly comparable to 1958 (−13, Eisenhower) and exceeds 1994 (−8) only if Democrats win both AK and MS (−9). Newsweek ran "Where Trump's Huge Midterms Defeat Ranks Among Worst in History" [A-44]. Precise historical rankings come from background knowledge and are flagged as such.
- **House:** At NBC's projection (+35 to +44), 2026 would be in the same tier as 2018 (+41 D) and 1994. It would stay below 2010 (−63), which a Quartz comparison also notes [A-33]. A 258-seat Democratic House would be the largest since 1993 (NBC) [A-28].
- **Breadth:** Democrats flipped seats in states Trump won by wide margins (Texas, Kansas, South Carolina, Iowa, Ohio) and a governorship in Texas. A near-flip in Nebraska via an independent (Osborn at 43.9%) also points to an unusually broad swing [A-07]–[A-14], [A-21].

### 6.3 Where the comparisons break down
1. **Mid-decade redistricting** (§2.3) changed baselines in Texas, California, Missouri (disputed), North Carolina, Ohio, Utah and possibly Virginia. Part of the House swing comes from the maps. The Texas and California redraws were designed to roughly offset each other (+5 R vs. +5 D), but the other maps were not symmetric.
2. **Different baselines.** CBS reports Democrats held 214 seats going in, which reflects vacancies and specials. Other outlets compute net change from the 2024 result (215 D). These yield different "net gains."
3. **Open seats and retirements.** Many Senate races were open: TX (the Paxton primary), IA, NC, MI, NH, MN, IL, KY, AL, MT, OK, WY and LA. Two more had appointed incumbents (OH Husted, FL Moody, and SC's Darline Graham). Results in these races mix candidate effects with the national swing.
4. **Senate classes differ.** The Class II seats up in 2026 are not the seats contested in 1994, 2010 or 2018, so the number of seats exposed to defeat differs.
5. **Uncalled races.** The 47–68 uncalled House races, many in California and Washington with slow mail counts, mean the final net figures could move several seats.

## (c) Conflicts & uncertainties

1. **House seat totals.** Snapshots range from 228 to 242 called Democratic seats and from 47 to 68 uncalled races. Projections range from 245 (CNBC, MS NOW) through 251–268 (CBS) and 258 (NBC) to a possible 281 (NBC upper bound). The causes are snapshot timing, AP versus in-house decision desks, and the 214 vs. 215 baseline [A-28]–[A-30].
2. **House net gain.** Figures of +18 (Ballotpedia floor), +25 (TEGNA/AP called), +31 (Quartz/TIME projection top), +35 (NBC called) and "more than three dozen" (Yahoo) all coexist. They are not all measuring the same thing.
3. **Senate count.** CNBC's earlier "at least 51" versus NBC and AP's 54 is a timing difference.
4. **Maine.** One Fox snapshot at 90.55% showed Collins ahead. AP, NBC and ABC show Jackson ahead, and Collins conceded. The Fox snapshot is probably stale or an artifact.
5. **Michigan.** One CBS heading still read "likely Democrat" while other outlets had called the race.
6. **North Carolina call time.** 8:15 p.m. vs. 8:25 p.m.
7. **Texas history claim.** Outlets disagree on whether this is the first Democratic Texas Senate win since 1988 or 1994. The 1988 date matches Bentsen; the 1994 framing appears in NBC text.
8. **South Carolina.** The "Darline Graham" name and the report of Lindsey Graham's death are consistent across several major outlets in the search summaries, but one summary flagged the name as a possible typo. Verify independently.
9. **Thune quote.** "Will not stand idly by" is unconfirmed.
10. **Mississippi.** The margin changed from 28 to 43 to 65 votes across snapshots. Thousands of ballots still need verification. Expect possible litigation or a judicial recount request.
11. **Alaska.** The count moved back and forth. The ranked-choice outcome depends on second preferences, including those of the second "Sullivan" candidate's voters. Certification is about Nov 25, per a single source.
12. **Redistricting.** Sources conflict on whether the Texas, Missouri and Virginia maps were used in 2026 [A-34].
13. **Turnout.** The 2018 baseline varies by source. The 2026 figure is preliminary and will rise as West Coast mail ballots are counted.
14. **Certification.** No result in this file is certified. All calls are media projections.
