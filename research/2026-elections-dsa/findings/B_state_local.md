# B: State legislatures, ballot measures, and local races, 2026 U.S. general election (excluding Washington State)

**Compiled:** 2026-11-04 (day after the election). **Research window:** roughly the morning through afternoon of Nov. 4, ET.
**Status of all results below:** election-night or day-after returns plus media and AP race calls. **No results have been certified.** Certification deadlines fall later in November and December depending on the state. Recounts are noted where a source mentions one.

**Method limitations. Read these first.**
- WebFetch was blocked for this session's network. ncsl.org, dlcc.substack.com and statecapitallobbyist.com all failed DNS resolution. Every figure below therefore comes from **WebSearch result summaries** of the cited pages, not from reading the full pages directly. Where a summary gave only a vague figure, I record it as vague. These numbers need to be verified against the primary pages.
- The session-wide WebSearch budget (200 calls, shared across all agents) ran out partway through this work. Ranked-choice voting, minimum wage, labor, health care, education/vouchers, redistricting (November), and most big-city local races are therefore **largely not covered**. See "Unverified/not found."
- Few search summaries gave exact publication times. Dates come from the URL or article dateline. "Wed" means Wed. Nov. 4, 2026.

**Trifectas.** An NBC News live blog citing AP says Democrats won governor plus both chambers in **Michigan, Minnesota, Nevada, Pennsylvania and Wisconsin** [B-14]. NCSL also says NC Democrats "gain a trifecta." That conflicts with WFMY's report that Republicans **kept** the NC Senate [B-9][B-15]. **Partisan-committee claims (labeled as such)**
- **DLCC (D committee)**, Nov 4: "12+ key chambers" flipped and "more than 450" GOP-held seats flipped. It claims the Texas House (≥21 seats), **both Arizona chambers** ("first Democratic trifecta since the 1960s"), the Georgia House, and 4 Georgia Senate seats. An earlier DLCC release that night claimed ">300 seat flips, at least ten majorities" [B-12].
- **RSLC (R committee)** statement: did not list holds. It described a hard national environment, said Republicans entered "at a high watermark," and blamed Democratic spending [B-16].
- Independent counts differ. State Capitol Lobbyist: "at least seven" chambers flipped, all to D (PA Senate, WI Assembly and Senate, NC House, NH House and Senate, MN House) [B-17]. NCSL adds the MI House and TX House [B-15]. A Poliwave live tracker showed "no chamber changed hands," but this was an early, outdated snapshot [B-18].

### 2g. Cross-cutting patterns (same electorate, divergent results)
- **Nevada:** voters approved abortion rights (Q6, ~63%) and elected a D Assembly supermajority. The voter-ID result (Q7) was not found [B-25][B-21].
- **North Carolina:** voters flipped the state House to Democrats while approving a **conservative voter-ID amendment (~60%)** and an **income-tax cap (~62%)** [B-9][B-28].
- **Massachusetts:** voters approved same-day registration (progressive) and **rejected open primaries**. They kept legal cannabis and the 2024 gun law (status quo on both), likely rejected changing the tax-refund formula, and split evenly on upzoning [B-30].
- **Colorado:** voters **rejected ICE cooperation** (Amd 81) but **approved tougher fentanyl and trafficking penalties** [B-33][B-34].
- **Nationally (Stateline):** voters backed tougher crime and bail measures while rejecting marijuana rollbacks and ICE-cooperation expansion. Five of six states approved citizen-only voting language. Arizona was the exception [B-29][B-34].
- **California:** voters were rejecting a billionaire wealth tax (Prop 40). NBC's tracker showed a voter-ID/citizenship amendment passing (unverified) [B-27][B-31].
- **Florida:** voters approved a large property-tax cut (Amd 3) [B-32].
- **Missouri:** voters kept abortion rights and in the same vote blocked a constitutional ban on gender-affirming care for minors [B-24].

## Conflicts & uncertainties
1. **Number of flipped chambers.** DLCC (partisan) says "12+." NCSL lists about 10 confirmed (PA S, WI S/A, NH H/S, MN H, MI H, NC H, TX H) plus GA House leading. State Capitol Lobbyist says "at least 7." Poliwave showed 0 (stale). Arizona (both chambers) and the Georgia House are **uncalled** as of Wed morning [B-12][B-13][B-11][B-15][B-17][B-18].
2. **Texas House seat count.** Net D gains are reported as ~21 (Tribune, DLCC), ≥18 (The Texan), and 13 seats undecided in one tally. A Wikipedia snippet claims R gains, which is stale or erroneous. The Daily Kos "99–81 → 92–88" figure fits Georgia's 180-seat House, not Texas's 150, and is probably misattributed in the search summary [B-10][B-12].
3. **Michigan House.** AP shows 60 D / 45 R with 5 unprojected. The House Democratic caucus claims 63 [B-8].
4. **Minnesota House.** Counts of 72, 75 and 77 DFL seats depend on the source and time. HD 32A recount pending [B-7].
5. **North Carolina trifecta.** The NCSL summary says Ds "gain a trifecta," but WFMY reports the R Senate majority survived by 2 seats [B-9][B-15].
6. **Arizona Prop 144.** The Hill snippet's 56% yes contradicts its "fail" headline and VisaVerge's 46.8% yes. Stateline says rejected [B-29].
7. **Colorado Amd 81.** Yes share given as 26.41% (Denver Gazette early) vs 44.8% (Colorado Sun 11:57 pm). Fails either way under the 55% threshold. AP reports failure [B-33].
8. **Idaho HJR 4.** 66.2% no vs 67.19% no, from different reporting cutoffs [B-34][B-35].
9. **NH Senate exact seats** and **NV Senate supermajority** are still pending [B-6][B-21].
10. **Iowa.** The "ends GOP control of the legislature" wording (Stateline) is ambiguous. IPR headline says Senate Republicans maintain their majority. Iowa House outcome not found [B-20][B-22].
11. **NBC tracker entries** (VT abortion ~62%, CA citizenship/ID ~64%, TN property tax) showed 0 vote counts and are **unverified** [B-27][B-32].
12. **Certification.** None of these results are certified as of Nov 4, 2026. AP calls, where noted, are not certification.
