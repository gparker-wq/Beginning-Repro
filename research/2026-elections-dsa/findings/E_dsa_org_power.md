## 1. DSA's stated goals and structure

**[E-1] Structure (general).** DSA's highest body is the biennial National Convention. Between conventions it is led by the National Political Committee (NPC). The NPC sets national endorsement criteria, and it votes on national endorsements after a recommendation from the National Electoral Commission (NEC) Steering Committee. A campaign must have a local chapter endorsement before it can be considered for a national one. *Source: search excerpts of Tempest and Red Star Caucus material on DSA endorsement procedure. The constitution/bylaws text was not opened (dsausa.org blocked).* Status: secondary, consistent across sources.

**[E-2] 2025 National Convention electoral resolution (CR05).** This consensus resolution came from the National Electoral Committee. Reported provisions:
- It prioritizes DSA members as candidates.
- It asks endorsed candidates to "openly and proudly identify with DSA and Socialism" and encourage others to join DSA.
- It urges chapters to seek "a commitment to building a socialist slate and political independence."
- It establishes a **Socialists-in-Office network** to "support, organize, and collaborate with DSA members who hold elected government office."
- It says DSA should bring its "independent organization and program to races whether on a Democratic, independent, or third-party ballot line."
- Amendment CR05-A03 creates a "more robust" federal endorsement process: the NPC meets the candidates and considers input from the NEC and members.

*Sources: Rochester Red Star convention account (rocdsa.org); International Viewpoint; search summaries.* Status: secondary/factional. Exact text not verified.

**[E-3] Resolution 22, "For a Fighting Anti-Zionist DSA" (2025).** The ADL describes it as making anti-Zionism a binding litmus test for candidates seeking a DSA endorsement. International Viewpoint also ties a Palestine standard to endorsement eligibility. *Sources: ADL backgrounder (an advocacy source that opposes DSA on this issue); International Viewpoint.* Status: secondary. Exact wording not verified.

**[E-4] National endorsements are not the same as membership.**
- Whether DSA *membership* is required for a **national** endorsement was **not confirmed**. CR05 "prioritizes" members.
- Several chapters do require membership: Detroit requires candidates to join; Sonoma requires membership plus joining the Socialists in Office committee after winning; Louisville calls membership the "best assurance."
- DSA-LA petitions show whether a candidate committed to running as a democratic socialist and joining Socialists in Office.

*Sources: chapter policy pages (detroitdsa.nationbuilder.com, socodsa.org/guidelines, dsa-la.org roadmap, sites.google.com/view/dsalouisville).* Status: primary (chapter level).

**[E-5] Socialists in Office committees.**
- NYC-DSA runs established state and city Socialists in Office (SIO) committees. Endorsed winners in the NY Legislature or the NYC Council are expected to join them (Wikipedia entries "New York State Socialists in Office" and "New York City Socialists in Office"; City & State field guide, July 2025).
- At the federal level, the **2024 conditional national endorsement of AOC** included conditions such as participating in a federal committee. The national body **withdrew** that endorsement in July 2024, citing her Israel positions and saying it had "not seen evidence of AOC meeting these conditions." NYC-DSA kept its own endorsement. *Sources: The Hill (July 2024), City & State (July 2024), Colorado Politics, Forward.*
- An Aug 17, 2026 Socialist Call piece (DSA-aligned) and a Substack report that the NPC set up a **Federal Socialists in Office / "Socialists in Congress" committee** "to facilitate communication, joint actions, endorsements, and strategy." **Not confirmed in any DSA primary document I could reach.** No membership rules or member list were found.

**[E-6] Party strategy debate ("dirty break" vs. "clean break").**
- The YDSA definition of the "dirty break": run on the Democratic ballot line while building infrastructure to run independently later.
- A "Clean Break: Declaring Independence from the Democrats" resolution, backed by the Just Break Already caucus, **did not get enough signatures to reach the 2025 convention floor.**
- The adopted CR05 language is ballot-line agnostic (see E-2).

*Sources: Just Break Already Caucus Substack (May 2025); YDSA, via search excerpt; ISA-linked critique.* Status: factional sources, but consistent with each other. **Documented:** DSA has not adopted a resolution to form an independent party or to stop using the Democratic line.

**[E-7] Platform.** The 2021 convention adopted a national political platform with planks on ecosocialism, internationalism, economic justice, D.C. statehood and "Deepening and Strengthening Democracy." The plank text was **not verified** (search did not return the adopted PDF). The claim that it calls for **abolishing the Senate** comes from Fox News questioning Schumer in July 2026 (see E-14) and is **not confirmed** from the platform text. **[PRIOR KNOWLEDGE – not re-verified]:** the 2021 platform includes Medicare for All, a Green New Deal, abolishing ICE, and structural democracy reforms. Washington Socialist (MDC-DSA, Sept 2021) calls the platform "the most significant outcome" of the 2021 convention.

**[E-8] Membership claims (organization's own figures).**
- **About 50,700** when Mamdani launched his mayoral run in Oct 2024 (Newsweek).
- **"Passed 120,000"** on **July 4, 2026**, which DSA called the largest socialist organization in U.S. history, surpassing the Debs-era Socialist Party's 1912 peak (AMAC/Newsweek, reporting DSA's claim).
- Wikipedia says **">130,000" in 2026**. Its NYC-DSA entry says **15,793** members as of July 2026.

Status: self-reported, not independently audited. The 120k and 130k figures conflict.

**[E-9] Scale of endorsements.** Brookings (Kamarck, Mahtani, Kraemer; Aug 19, 2026) counted **282** candidates endorsed or recommended by official DSA chapters, of whom **29** had a *national* DSA endorsement. Secondary summaries report:

- **90** of the candidates were in California and **21** in New York.
- Only **10** won in districts less Democratic than D+10.

## 2. Contrast with the DNC and Democratic leadership

**[E-10] DNC structure. [PRIOR KNOWLEDGE – not re-verified this session].**
- The DNC Charter and Bylaws require the national chair and officers to stay **impartial and evenhanded in presidential nominating contests** (Charter Art. 5, Sec. 4). This rule covers the presidential process, not congressional primaries.
- The DNC funds state parties and coordinated campaigns. In congressional races, DCCC/DSCC "red to blue" and incumbent-protection programs are separate.
- The 2024 platform was adopted Aug 2024 at the Chicago convention.
- Ken Martin was elected DNC chair on Feb 1, 2025.

## 4. Institutional power in the new Congress: margin arithmetic

**[E-21] House (preliminary).**
- AP as of 12:30 p.m. ET Nov 4: **D 229 called, R 139, 67 uncalled** (State Capital Lobbyist, citing AP). CBS8: 228–139, 68 uncalled. 270toWin/DDHQ: 36 uncalled at 11:40 a.m.
- NBC projects **≥258 D**. CNBC: ≥225 D.
- The chamber entered the election at 218 R / 214 D. Democrats gained about 25 seats (Newsweek, CBS8).
- California counts ballots that arrive through Nov 10.

**[E-22] Senate.** Democrats are projected to control the chamber with at least 51 seats (AP via PBS; CNBC). They started at R 53 / D 45 (plus 2 independents). Two races were uncalled (Alaska, Mississippi), and outlets disagreed on Michigan and South Carolina. DSA has no senators.

**[E-23] Is the DSA bloc pivotal? (arithmetic)**
- **Passing a bill:** a majority of members voting is needed, which is 218 with all 435 voting. At the **current called floor of 229 D**, Democrats can lose **11** votes on a party-line bill, assuming every Republican votes no and everyone attends (229 − 218 = 11). The DSA-connected bloc is **8** (E-16). **Voting no alone, it cannot defeat a party-line bill (8 < 11).**
  - At 8 no votes, Democrats would have 221 yes against at least 214 no (if every uncalled seat went Republican, R = 206 + 8 = 214), so the bill passes.
  - The bloc becomes **decisive only if at least 4 other Democrats defect alongside it** (8 + 4 = 12 > 11).
  - The bloc's leverage disappears entirely as the majority grows. At NBC's ≥258, Democrats could lose 40.
  - **"Present" votes:** if the bloc votes present instead of no, the threshold drops to 214 (427 voting), and the arithmetic shows them even less able to block.
- **Speaker election:** a candidate needs a majority of members voting *for a person by name*. At 229 D with all Republicans voting for their own candidate, Jeffries can afford 11 Democratic defections who vote for someone else. If all 8 bloc members voted "present", the 427 voting would set the threshold at 214, and Jeffries would have 221. **The bloc alone cannot deny Jeffries the gavel at 229 or more Democratic seats.** Only a combined progressive holdout of at least 12 could, and E-13 reports wider "not committed" sentiment among some progressives.
- **Where the bloc could matter (inference):** votes where moderate Democrats cross to the GOP, such as Israel/Gaza, crypto, immigration enforcement or "condemn socialism" resolutions (E-15: 8 Democrats crossed). Votes needing a **two-thirds suspension** (290 of 435). Bills that Republicans oppose en bloc while the 120th Congress's margin tightens through vacancies. Committee ratios and leadership elections inside the caucus (the CPC's 101 is 44% of a 229-member caucus).
- **Caucus sizes (pre-election):**
  - New Democrat Coalition: about 112 (GovTrack, Feb 2026, "may be incomplete"); 110 at the start of the 119th Congress (Wikipedia). Largest ideological caucus.
  - CPC: 101 (Sept 2026).
  - Blue Dogs: 10 (Wikipedia/GovTrack 2026) vs. 18 (dems.gov, Ballotpedia; likely stale).
  - **Post-election sizes: not found as of Nov 4.** The caucuses overlap and do not add up to the total.

**[E-24] Prior conduct on close votes. [PRIOR KNOWLEDGE – not re-verified this session; search budget exhausted]:**
- **March 2025 GOP full-year CR:** passed the House 217–213. AOC and Tlaib voted no with nearly all Democrats; only Rep. Jared Golden (D) voted yes. Here DSA members voted *with* the party.
- **Nov 2025 CR ending the Oct 1 – Nov 12, 2025 shutdown:** passed the House about 222–209 with 6 Democratic yes votes. AOC and Tlaib voted no, again with the party majority.
- **Sept 2021 Iron Dome supplemental:** 420–9. Tlaib voted no; AOC switched to "present." This is a break with the party.
- **Nov 2023 censure of Tlaib:** 234–188 with 22 Democrats voting yes.
- **Pattern (inference):** on budget and shutdown votes, DSA members have mostly voted with Democratic leadership against GOP bills. Their breaks have mostly come on Israel/Gaza and defense, and occasionally on final passage of the NDAA.
- **Not verified this session:** NDAA, GENIUS/crypto and Laken Riley roll calls.
