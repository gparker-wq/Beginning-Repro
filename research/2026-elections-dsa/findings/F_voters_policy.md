### 1B. Topline findings (early, subject to reweighting)

- CNN's Harry Enten gave 38% approve / 62% disapprove and called it the worst net midterm approval for any president in the exit or voter-poll era.
- CBS's comparison series (relayed by a poll-tracker account): Bush 2006 43%, Obama 2010 44%, Trump 2018 45%, Biden 2022 44%, Trump 2026 38%.
- NBC: 45% approved in 2018.

**[F-9]** Fox News Voter Poll:

## Part 4 — What the data can and cannot establish

**What these data CAN establish (with stated error):**

- **The composition and stated attitudes of the electorate**, for example "about 4 in 10 voters named the economy as the top issue."
  - Accuracy depends on the sample design. Probability-based components plus large samples (about 79k for NBC, about 100k for AP) give small sampling error nationally, roughly ±1 point.
  - State and subgroup estimates carry much wider error, ±3–6 points or more for small groups such as Muslim voters in Michigan or young men.
- **Correlations between stated attitudes and stated vote**, for example "about 8 in 10 of those naming democracy as top issue voted Democratic."
- **Change over time**, when the question wording and design are the same (NBC 2018 vs 2026 approval).
  - Comparisons across products are fragile. VoteCast (NORC) to the AP Voter Poll (SSRS) was a design change; exit polls and VoteCast differed in 2024.
- **Stated motive**, such as 43% voting "to express opposition to Trump."
  - This is self-reported and shaped by question wording and social desirability.

**What these data CANNOT establish:**

1. **Individual causal motive.** That voters who disapprove of tariffs voted Democratic does not show tariffs *caused* their vote. Attitudes are bundled with party identification, and partisans tend to rationalize. Ranking issues as "crucial" or "major" (Iran: 34% / 40%) is not a causal estimate.
2. **Ecological inference.** Inferring individual behavior from area-level results is invalid. Examples: "Muslim-heavy precincts swung, so Muslims voted X," or "socialist candidates won Democratic seats, so voters endorse socialism." Precinct data cannot say which individuals moved. DSA House winners mostly ran in safe-blue seats, where a Democratic nominee would win almost regardless of ideology. Their wins do not by themselves measure support for socialism.
3. **That the election was a mandate for specific policies.** NBC's own reading was "no progressive earthquake": issue views were roughly unchanged, and socialism stayed net-negative ([F-23]). The result is consistent with anti-incumbent and economic backlash. It does not by itself show endorsement of Medicare for All or other platform items.
4. **Final numbers.** Early exit polls are weighted to pre-election expectations, then reweighted to actual results. Subgroup figures, such as Latino 62% D and young men +33 D, often move several points. Figures in this file should be re-checked against final releases, typically one to two weeks out, and against later validated-voter studies (Pew validated voters, Catalist, CES), which often differ from exit polls on group shares.
5. **Comparisons with prior years across vendors.** "Young men moved from Trump +15 to D +33" may compare a 2024 source with a 2026 source that used different methods.
6. **Pre-election poll error.** Polls have house effects and turnout-model error; Florida Senate polls ranged from Moody +2 to +7. CNN's own Nov 2 piece discussed response-rate problems; its details were not retrieved.
7. **Favorability of the word versus policy support.** "Socialism" favorability depends heavily on definition and context. Cato did not define the term, and NBC (21% positive) and Gallup (43% positive) differ widely because of wording, sample (registered voters vs adults) and response options. Policy items also swing with framing: KFF Medicare for All support was 66% unprompted versus 64% opposed if taxes rise.

### 5A. Federal

**[F-46] [PROMISE/BILL] House Democrats' agenda.** Jeffries is expected to become Speaker; he must win the floor vote when the new Congress convenes Jan 3, 2027.
- Planned in three tranches: the first 100 hours, the first 100 days, and the first year.
- Early votes planned on:
  - restoring the expired enhanced ACA tax credits;
  - reversing Medicaid cuts;
  - ending Trump's global tariffs and restoring congressional power over trade;
  - opposing the Iran war.
- Five affordability working groups: housing; health care; groceries and goods; gas and utilities; caregiving.
- Committee oversight investigations focused on corruption.
- Impeachment: Jeffries said "we haven't ruled anything in, and we haven't ruled anything out."
- Sources: ABC News, NBC News, PBS.

**[F-47] [FORECAST] Constraints on that agenda.**

*Veto override.*
- An override needs two-thirds of each chamber.
- With 54 Democratic senators, an override needs 13 Republicans. One analysis says it would still take about 11 even if the uncalled races go Democratic.
- In the House, about 290 votes are needed. If the reported figure of 228 Democrats holds ([F-48]), that means about 62 Republicans. This is my arithmetic.

*Filibuster.*
- Ending debate takes 60 votes, so about 6 Republicans at 54 seats.
- Budget reconciliation could pass ACA-subsidy restoration by simple majority (Insurance Business, forecast). It would still face a veto.
- Ending the filibuster is advocacy, not a reported plan.

*Tariffs.*
- The Supreme Court ruled in February 2026 that IEEPA does not authorize tariffs, according to one secondary source. Verify this.
- The administration then shifted to Section 301 and similar delegated authorities.
- Congress can repeal those delegations only by statute, which is subject to a veto. Effective repeal is unlikely within this term without Republican votes (Bloomberg/BNN, Rio Times).

*Leverage Democrats do have.*
- Senate confirmation of nominees.
- Appropriations and the power of the purse.
- Oversight subpoenas.
- War-powers resolutions, which are also vetoable.

*Who would be affected.*
- ACA marketplace enrollees who lost the enhanced subsidies.
- Medicaid enrollees subject to the 2025 cuts.
- Importers and consumers paying tariffs.
- Fiscal estimates: no CBO score of a 2027 ACA-subsidy restoration or a tariff repeal was found as of 2026-11-04.

**[F-48] [OBSERVED, preliminary] Chamber control.**
- Fox (Nov 4): Democrats at 54 Senate seats and 228 House seats, with Alaska's Senate race uncalled.
- NBC: up to 7 Senate flips and at least 29 House flips.
- Tax Foundation: "What a Democratic Congress Means for Federal Tax Policy in 2027"; contents not retrieved.
- Seat counts are not final and other researchers cover them. A Washington Examiner newsletter headlined "The arrival of divided government" refers to Democratic Congress versus Republican president.

### 5B. New York City — Mayor Mamdani (took office Jan 2026)

#### Rent freeze — [ENACTED] Jun 25, 2026

**[F-49]**
- The Rent Guidelines Board voted 0% increases on both one- and two-year leases for about 1 million rent-stabilized apartments. Mamdani appointed 6 of the board's 9 members.
- It is the first-ever freeze on two-year leases.
- In May, the board's preliminary range was 0–2% for one-year leases and 0–4% for two-year leases.
- The previous year's increases were 3% (one-year) and 4.5% (two-year).
- The board's own research found landlord costs rose about 5.3% from 2025 to 2026.
- REBNY condemned the decision, and a legal challenge was expected.
- **[OBSERVED]** Effects on tenants, maintenance and owner finances: not found as of 2026-11-04. Leases that began after Oct 1, 2026 are the first affected, so it is too early for outcome data.

#### Free buses — [PROMISE, not enacted]

**[F-50]**
- In April 2026, Mamdani conceded that citywide fare-free buses would not happen in 2026.
- The Assembly's one-house budget proposed $15M for one fare-free line per borough. The Senate backed reviving the earlier pilot.
- The enacted outcome was not confirmed.
- **[FORECAST]** Cost estimates:
  - administration: about $700M a year;
  - MTA chair Lieber: about $1B, including the extra service needed;
  - advocate Charles Komanoff: about $800M.
- **[OBSERVED]** The 2023–24 five-route pilot: ridership rose, with about 50,000 more rides, but forgone revenue estimates conflict ($12M vs $16.5M). The claimed safety benefits are statistically weak (City Journal).
- A World Cup fare-free period, pegged at about $100M, was not confirmed to have happened.
- Mamdani appointed a senior adviser for fast and free buses in May 2026.

#### Childcare ("2-Care") — [ENACTED, partial]

**[F-51]**

**[F-53]**
