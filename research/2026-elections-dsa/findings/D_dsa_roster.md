# D. DSA-Connected Candidates & Officeholders, 2026: Roster and Win/Loss Analysis

Researcher D. Compiled 2026-11-04 (day after the Nov 3, 2026 general election). **Data cutoff: about midday ET, Nov 4, 2026.**

## Method and limits (read first)

- **How sources were read.** All evidence came from web-search result snippets and the search tool's summaries of them. WebFetch could not reach the primary sites: `npr.org` and `democraticleft.dsausa.org` failed DNS resolution, so this session's network allowlist blocks them. No article was read in full. Numbers here are what search snippets reported. Treat each one as "reported by X," not as a figure checked against the original page.
- **Search budget.** The shared WebSearch budget (200 calls per turn, across all six researchers) ran out partway through this work. Several planned lookups were never run. They are listed in section (f). Nothing was filled in from guesswork.
- **Background knowledge.** A few items come from my own training knowledge (cutoff June 2026), not from a source found this session. They are tagged **[BK]** and are unverified here.
- **Fact vs. inference.** **[FACT]** means a documented claim from a cited source. **[INF]** means my inference.
- **Advocacy and partisan outlets** are labeled where cited:
  - Daily Signal: conservative advocacy (Heritage-affiliated).
  - Fox News: conservative-leaning.
  - The National Pulse, Breitbart, Townhall: right-wing partisan.
  - WSWS: Trotskyist, and hostile to DSA.
  - Democratic Left and chapter blogs: DSA's own publications (self-interested).
  - Jacobin and Democracy Now: left-leaning.

## (c) Win/loss analysis

**Big picture (documented).** DSA's congressional caucus grows from 2 to 8:
- 6 new members: Valdez, Avila Chevalier, Kiros, McKinney, Rabb, Gonzales-Torres.
- 2 incumbents: AOC and Tlaib.
- Sources: [D-01, D-04]. Tlaib's general-election win is **NF** (presumed [INF]).

Of 8 DSA-endorsed non-incumbent House candidates:
- 6 won.
- 1 lost (Jewett, LA-1).
- 1 trails, uncalled (Vang, CA-7).

Below the House:
- Fox analysis of the DSA tracker: 60 of 161 contests won so far, up to 106 if leads hold [D-39].
- DSA claims at least 71 candidates and measures won [D-03, D-39].

**Test 1: was it the general-election wave? Mostly no for the House pickups, because the decisive contests were primaries or intra-Democratic top-two races.** [INF on documented numbers]
- Every DSA House winner won a seat where the November general was no real contest:
  - Kiros 78% [D-14]
  - Valdez 83% [D-08]
  - Avila Chevalier ~87% [D-12]
  - Rabb 94%, with no Republican [D-18]
  - McKinney, Republican at ~22% in a partial count [D-16]
  - Gonzales-Torres, Democrat-vs-Democrat [D-19]
- This matches Brookings's pre-election finding [D-40]:
  - Only 10 DSA-backed candidates won in districts with a Cook PVI Democratic lean under 10 points.
  - "As a state or district becomes more competitive, fewer DSA-endorsed candidates run, and fewer win."
  - Its overall framing: "more like a skirmish than a war."
- Implication: the national Democratic wave (Democrats took both chambers [D-06]) did **not** decide the House pickups. Primary-electorate dynamics did.
- The one DSA candidate in hostile territory, Jewett, lost by ~27 points [D-23].
- Gap: overperformance vs. the 2024 presidential baseline could **not** be computed (PVI and 2024 margins NF).

**Test 2: was it incumbent weakness or candidate quality? Strong evidence in several races.** [INF]
- Three primary unseatings by DSA-endorsed candidates: Kiros, Avila Chevalier, McKinney (Ballotpedia: "three DSA-endorsed candidates defeated Democratic incumbents in primaries") [D-26]. Gonzales-Torres makes a fourth incumbent defeat, in November.
- Margins in two of the three were narrow (3.8 points each):
  - NY-13: an incumbent who had won easily before [BK].
  - MI-13: Thanedar's money advantage [D-15].
- Local factors can explain narrow margins:
  - Gomez's ethics investigation [D-19].
  - Thanedar's weak standing [BK] and the Israel-aid debate [D-15].
  - DeGette's long tenure and corporate-PAC record [D-13].
- Candidate quality:
  - Kiros won by a non-narrow 13 points and dominated the party assembly [D-13].
  - Rabb (an experienced state legislator) won a three-way open-seat race by ~15 [D-17].
- Taken together, NBC framed DeGette's loss as "a dangerous political environment for Democratic incumbents" [D-13 list]. That is an anti-incumbent/anti-establishment mood within Democratic primaries, overlapping with (but not identical to) DSA organizing.

**Test 3: was it DSA organizing specifically? Plausible but not separable on available data.** [INF]
- Chapter endorsements came with volunteer commitments. DSA-LA's endorsement "commits... volunteers, canvassers" [D-36].
- Volunteer and door-knock counts: **NF**. DSA's own claims were not retrieved.
- Coalition endorsements overlap heavily (WFP, JD, OR, Mamdani), so DSA's marginal effect cannot be isolated. Examples: Rabb (WFP + JD) [D-17]; Gonzales-Torres (JD + OR) [D-20].

**Test 4: issue positions (Israel/Gaza) and the "Mamdani effect."** [mixed]
- Forward: all DSA members heading to Congress called Gaza a genocide and support cutting military aid. It also says these positions are increasingly held by non-DSA Democrats [D-41].
- Control case: Abdul El-Sayed (not DSA; "capitalist") won the Michigan Senate race 52–45 [D-03, D-06].
  - **[INF]** Left-populist and Israel-critical positions can win statewide without the DSA label. The label is not necessary.
- Nixon (DSA member) lost Florida by ~3 [D-24]. Plausibly a large overperformance vs. 2024 [BK]. No same-ballot comparison was made (NF), so it cannot be attributed to DSA.
- Mamdani endorsed both NYC House winners [D-07, D-11]. **[INF]** The NYC wins likely reflect the post-2025 Mamdani organizing infrastructure.

**Test 5: anti-Trump sentiment.**
- The DC mayoral race (82–84%) and Providence (81%) are lopsided generals in deep-blue cities [D-27, D-38].
- Trump's threat to take over DC [D-28] may have nationalized that race. No polling was retrieved: **NF**.

**Net assessment [INF].** The best explanation is a combination:
1. A deep-blue-seat strategy (a documented pattern).
2. Weak or scandal-hit incumbents and open seats.
3. An anti-establishment Democratic primary electorate.
4. DSA/WFP/JD/Mamdani coalition organizing.
5. Israel-aid positioning resonant with primary voters.

1. **How many DSA House winners: 4, 5, 6, 7 or 8.** The counts differ in two ways: what is being counted, and when it was counted.

   - "6": NPR's updated headline. New members, including Gonzales-Torres after AP's Wednesday-morning call.
   - "7": Daily Signal "including AOC and Tlaib," before CA-34.
   - "8": DSA's live blog (6 new + AOC + Tlaib).
   - Possible "9": Davis Vanguard's pre-election ceiling, if Vang wins.
   - None of these counts treats Casar or Lee as DSA.

   - House: 10 (tracker, Daily Signal, Ballotpedia) vs. 11 (Rose Garden DSA).
