# C — WASHINGTON STATE: 2026 General Election (Nov 3, 2026)

**Researcher scope:** Washington statewide measures, county splits, 26th LD, legislature, congressional, local measures, DSA links.
**Compiled:** 2026-11-04 (day after election).
**Method note (important):** Direct page fetches were BLOCKED in this session (DNS/allowlist failure) for results.vote.wa.gov, washingtonstatestandard.com, kuow.org, knkx.org, gigharbornow.org. All findings below come from **web-search result snippets/summaries** of the cited pages, not from reading the pages in full. The shared web-search budget (200 calls per turn) ran out partway through, so several sub-questions are only partly answered (see "Unverified / not found"). Every number is **preliminary election-night or early-Wednesday data** unless stated otherwise. Washington votes entirely by mail, and ballots postmarked by Election Day still count, so late ballots (which often lean left in Seattle/King County) will move these numbers for days.

**Key calendar [C-1]:** County canvassing boards certify on **Nov 24, 2026**. The Secretary of State must certify by **Dec 3, 2026** (SOS General Election Fact Sheet dated 10/01/2026; matching Thurston and Walla Walla county calendars). One WA State Standard voter-guide snippet mentioned "Dec. 11," which is apparently about a different certification. Treat Dec 3 as authoritative.

## 1. Statewide ballot measures

Three statewide measures were on the ballot, all **initiatives to the Legislature/People (initiated statutes)**. There were **no** legislatively referred constitutional amendments and **no** advisory votes (Washington abolished advisory votes in 2023). [C-2] (Ballotpedia News, Oct 6, 2026: "three ballot measures — all of which are initiated state statutes". An older Ballotpedia page saying "as of March 22, 2026, two measures certified" is out of date.)

### Why the 26th is a swing district [C-20]
- In 2022 it split its ticket, sending a Democrat to the Senate and Republicans to both House seats. Margins in 2022 were under 1.3 points in two races, and the Pos. 1 lead flipped during the count.
- In 2024 it split again (D Pos. 1 by about 3.7 points, R Pos. 2 by about 9.4 points).
- It mixes suburban/exurban Gig Harbor (Pierce) with Port Orchard and south Kitsap, plus a military/shipyard workforce near Bremerton. It was Emily Randall's base before her 2024 win in WA-6.
- **Money:** It is one of the most expensive legislative districts in WA. The 2025 Senate special drew about **$4 million** in total. Candidates spent about $1.4M (Krishnadasan raised more than $800K, Caldier about $706K). Outside groups spent about $2.6M: roughly $1.33M against Krishnadasan, mostly from Sound Jobs and Washington Wins (Braun-linked), and about $630K against Caldier from New Direction PAC. [C-21]
- **2026 spending (PDC):** Not retrieved. The PDC data page appeared in search, but no 2026 LD26 figures could be confirmed (a "$873,402.02 LD26 House 2" figure was on a stale snapshot of unknown year, so it is **not** used).

## 4. Legislature: notable races and control

**Pre-election baseline [C-22]:** House 59 D – 39 R (98 seats). Senate 30 D – 19 R (49 seats); 24 Senate seats were up in 2026. A two-thirds supermajority means **66 House seats** (D need +7) and **33 Senate seats** (D need +3). With a supermajority, Democrats could override gubernatorial vetoes and refer constitutional amendments without Republican votes.

**Election-night picture [C-23]:** The WA State Standard reported early counts showing **Democrats flipping 9 Republican-held House seats**. That would exceed the 7 needed for a House supermajority, but the count is unconfirmed. Democratic consultant Dean Nielsen called it "historically bad" for the WA GOP. GOP chair Jim Walsh said it was too early to call it a blue wave. KNKX headline (11/4): "Dems approaching supermajority in WA state Legislature." **No final seat count is available.** Both chambers stay Democratic (NBC lists Senate control: Democrats).

## 6. Cross-cutting results (same electorate, split verdicts)

1. **Statewide:** Voters **kept a progressive income tax** (I-645 repeal failing 41.9/58.1) and **rejected the parents'-rights initiative** (47.3/52.7). The same voters **approved the trans-sports restriction** (56.8/43.2). Roughly 13–16% of the electorate must have voted to keep the tax and also for I-638. KUOW's framing: "socially conservative, fiscally liberal." [C-3–5][C-8]
2. **Spokane County:** It kept the millionaires tax (54.67% No on repeal) but **backed I-001 parents' rights (~54% Yes)**, against the statewide result. [C-10]
3. **26th LD:** Republicans topped the August primary in both House seats (Olson, Cornell). In the general, Democrats led all three seats by about 10–17 points. Measure-level results for the LD were not found. [C-13–16]
4. **Eastern rural vs. college/wine counties:** Eight rural eastern counties voted to repeal the tax, while Whitman (WSU) and Walla Walla kept it. [C-11]
5. **Seattle:** A 72.6% yes vote on a regressive sales-tax increase for transit came alongside about 66.5% no on income-tax repeal countywide (King). Seattle-only I-638 numbers were not found. [C-9][C-37]
6. **Intra-Democratic:** Income-tax holdouts Walen (LD48) and Morgan (LD29) trailed left challengers. In LD32, the Senate race between two Democrats turned on *extending* the tax down to $250K, and the candidate favoring extension (Ryu) narrowly led. [C-27][C-28]

## Conflicts & uncertainties

1. **All 2026 numbers are election-night (11/3, ~8:30–9:17 PM SOS drop) or early 11/4.** No later drop was captured. WA counts continue through Nov 24 (county certification), and late ballots usually shift results left in King County.
2. **WA-5 lead may have flipped.** NWPB and the SOS snippet show Baumgartner +1,857 (8:40 PM), but the Spokesman-Review URL slug reads "conroy-narrowly-leads…" (an earlier version), and its later headline says "Baumgartner narrowly leads." Both states are recorded.
3. **LD26 Senate snapshot** (26,342 votes) is not comparable with the House totals (about 56,850). It is probably partial or from one county.
4. **LD26 Pos. 2 incumbent identity:** Sources call the retiring Republican incumbent "Michelle Valdez," but Michelle **Caldier** (R) won Pos. 2 in 2024. Possibilities include a name change or a resignation/appointment, but this was **not resolved**. Verify before publishing.
5. **LD26 Senate primary:** 56.7/43.3 vs 56.09/43.83. Probably a different count date.
6. **2022 Pos. 1 margin:** 735 (SOS/Gig Harbor Now) vs 726 (Kitsap Daily News).
7. **Spokane County I-001 at ~54% Yes** comes from a single search summary of the county PDF and has not been verified.
8. **A "Randall trails Alan Nolan" claim** in one search summary was an AI-summary error. Nolan is an LD6 legislative candidate (vs Michaela Kelso). Randall (WA-6) leads Teresa Fox about 65–35.
9. **"9 GOP House seats flipped"** is an early WSS characterization. A supermajority is not confirmed.
10. **The I-645 constitutional lawsuit** is mentioned only in a summary. Its details and timing are unverified.
11. **Certification date:** Dec 3 (SOS) vs a WSS mention of "Dec. 11."
12. **Measure naming:** IP26-645 / IL26-638 / IL26-001 (SOS filing numbers) vs "Initiative 645/638/1" (media). These are the same measures.
