## 0. Read this first: status of the evidence

1. **Nothing here is certified.** Every result is an election-night count, a next-morning count, or a news-organization projection (AP, NBC, CBS, CNN, DDHQ). Known certification dates:
   - North Carolina canvass: Nov 13 [A-24].
   - Alaska ranked-choice tabulation: Nov 18; certification about Nov 25 [A-31].
   - Washington counties: Nov 24; state by Dec 3 [C-1].

2. **Primary pages were not read.** The session's network policy blocked direct fetches of dsausa.org, npr.org, nbcnews.com, cbsnews.com, ballotpedia.org, results.vote.wa.gov, ncsl.org and others. Nearly every figure therefore comes from **search-engine summaries of the cited pages**. Each figure should be read as "reported by X at time T" and checked against the original page before it is quoted.
3. **Search ran out.** A 200-call search budget was used up partway through, so specific gaps remain. They are listed per file under "Unverified / not found" and in §12 below.
4. **Primary DSA documents were not opened.** These include the constitution, the platform and the 2025 convention resolutions. The claims about them in §4 come from secondary and factional sources and are labeled as such.
5. **Some items come from model background knowledge** (cutoff June 2026). They are tagged `[BK]` or "PRIOR KNOWLEDGE – not re-verified" in the findings files and are not relied on for conclusions.
6. **Conflicting reports are preserved, not reconciled away.** Where outlets disagree (House seat counts, the number of DSA winners, Washington's WA-5, Arizona Prop 144, and others), every version is kept with its timestamp, and the likely reason for the discrepancy is noted.

## 3. Network and coordination (Q3)

**Documented:**
- **Overlapping endorsement networks.** Justice Democrats backed Kiros, Avila Chevalier, McKinney and Gonzales-Torres in their primaries. The WFP endorsed Rabb and Lewis George. Mamdani endorsed both New York City winners [E-17, D-07, D-11].
- **Shared field infrastructure in NYC.** NYC-DSA's June 2026 primary slate reports about 8,500 volunteers and 750,000–800,000+ doors knocked; these are the organization's own figures, and they differ by source. The operation carried over from Mamdani's 2025 campaign. Mamdani created a city Office of Mass Engagement in January 2026 and appointed a former NYC-DSA field organizer to lead it [E-18].
- **Formal structures.** The 2025 convention resolution (CR05) reportedly creates a Socialists-in-Office network. NYC-DSA runs established state and city Socialists in Office committees [E-2, E-5].
- **Public statements.** On election night Mamdani called on the new democratic socialists to "seize this moment" [E-20].

**Not documented / not found:**
- No **formal socialist caucus in Congress**. A Jacobin piece on Nov 4 argued that one is needed, which indicates none exists [E-19].
- A federal "Socialists in Congress" committee is reported only by DSA-aligned outlets [E-5b].
- No joint pledge by the 2026 winners to caucus or vote together.
- No evidence of shared consultants, staff, firms or joint fundraising committees. This was not fully searched.
- No co-sponsorship analysis was performed.

**Divergence among DSA-connected officials:**
- None of the incoming DSA-endorsed House members has committed to backing Jeffries for Speaker; Rabb said, "I am nobody's rubber stamp."
- AOC reportedly backs Jeffries, per a single WSJ-attributed report, and Mamdani backs him, per CNN [E-13].
- Nixon is reported to have joined DSA while saying she is not a socialist [E-16].
- Raman has a long-running dispute with DSA-LA over Israel-related endorsements [D-36].

**Assessment.** The 2026 results added a set of individuals linked by **shared endorsers and, in NYC, a shared field operation**. There is not yet a **documented, formally coordinated legislative bloc**. Whether one forms is a January 2027 question. Anything stronger than "networked through endorsements and field work" would be inference.

## 4. What the DSA is trying to accomplish, compared with the DNC (Q4)

**DSA's stated aims and structure.** These come from secondary and factional sources; the primary texts were not opened.
- **Structure.** The biennial convention is supreme. The National Political Committee (NPC) sets endorsement criteria and votes on national endorsements. A chapter endorsement is a prerequisite [E-1].
- **2025 electoral resolution CR05** [E-2]:
  - prioritizes member candidates;
  - asks endorsees to "openly and proudly identify with DSA and Socialism";
  - seeks "political independence";
  - creates a Socialists-in-Office network;
  - is **ballot-line agnostic**: Democratic, independent or third-party.
- **Resolution 22** reportedly makes anti-Zionism an endorsement standard [E-3; ADL characterization].
- **Independent party.** A "clean break" resolution to leave the Democratic line **failed to reach the 2025 floor**. DSA's working strategy is the "dirty break": use the Democratic line now while building independent capacity [E-6].
- **Platform.** The 2021 platform has planks on ecosocialism, internationalism and democracy reform [E-7]. Specific planks such as Medicare for All, abolishing ICE and the Green New Deal are `[PRIOR KNOWLEDGE]`. The "abolish the Senate" claim comes from Fox and was not verified.
- **Membership (self-reported):** about 50,700 in Oct 2024; "passed 120,000" on July 4, 2026. Wikipedia says more than 130,000 [E-8].

**DNC contrast.**
- The DNC is a party committee whose charter requires officer neutrality in **presidential** nominating contests. Its role is funding state parties and coordinated campaigns. This is `[PRIOR KNOWLEDGE]`; the charter text was not re-verified [E-10].
- Its goal is winning elections under a broad, non-socialist platform. DSA is a membership organization with an explicitly socialist program, internal accountability expectations for endorsees, and a stated long-term interest in independence.
- **DNC chair Ken Martin** defended DSA as part of a "big tent": "that's where the vast majority of Americans are at" (Oct 12, 2026) [E-11].
- **Jeffries:** "I don't support the DSA agenda as has been articulated by the DSA itself" (Aug 2026) [E-12].
- **Schumer** sidestepped the question [E-14].

**Stated goals compared with campaigns and governing:**
- **Where they ran.** Every 2026 House winner ran **in Democratic primaries in safe Democratic seats**. They campaigned mainly on affordability (rent, childcare, cost of living), corporate money, ICE, and Israel/Gaza. None ran on independence from the Democratic Party [D §b, E-25].
- **Israel/Gaza.** Forward reports that all of them described Gaza as a genocide and support cutting military aid [D-41].
- **Mamdani's NYC record is the clearest governing test** [E §5, F-49–F-54]:

| Promise | Status |
|---|---|
| Rent freeze | **Enacted** (Rent Guidelines Board, 7–1, June 25, 2026; about 1M units) |
| Childcare | **Partly enacted** (2-K, state-funded for two years; long-term financing unresolved) |
| Free buses | **Not enacted** |
| City grocery stores | **Funded, none open** (first targeted for 2027; lawsuit pending) |
| Millionaire tax | **Rejected by Albany**, which provided state aid and a luxury second-home tax instead |

The pattern is that **what the mayor controls directly was delivered, and what depends on Albany or large new revenue was not.**

## 5. Why DSA-connected candidates won or lost (Q5)

The full analysis is in [D §c]. The tests below are inferences built on documented numbers.

1. **The national Democratic wave did not decide the DSA House pickups.** Every winner's general election was uncompetitive: 78–94%, a seat with no Republican, or a Democrat-vs-Democrat race. The decisive contests were **primaries**, or California's **intra-Democratic top-two** general. This matches Brookings' pre-election finding that only 10 DSA-backed candidates had won in seats less Democratic than D+10, and that "as a district becomes more competitive, fewer DSA-endorsed candidates run, and fewer win" [D-40].
2. **Incumbent weakness and open seats mattered.**
   - Two primary upsets were narrow: NY-13 by 3.8 points and MI-13 by 3.8 points.
   - Gomez (CA-34) was under a House Ethics review.
   - DeGette had served 15 terms and was attacked over corporate PAC money.
   - Thanedar had a large money advantage and still lost.
   - Two of the six seats were open (NY-7, PA-3).
3. **Organizing is plausible but cannot be separated from the coalition.** Self-reported NYC field numbers are large, but DSA endorsements overlap almost completely with Justice Democrats, the WFP and Mamdani.
4. **Issue positioning helped in primaries, and the label is not required.**
   - Israel/Gaza positions resonated in primaries [D-15, D-41].
   - The **El-Sayed control case** shows a left-populist, Israel-critical candidate winning statewide in Michigan *without* any DSA tie.
5. **Results were weaker outside safe seats.**
   - Jewett lost by about 27 points in a deep-red district.
   - Nixon lost Florida by about 2.9 points. That is roughly a 10-point improvement on Trump's 2024 margin, but it was not compared with other Florida Democrats on the same ballot, so it cannot be credited to Nixon or DSA [D-24].
   - Vang trails in CA-7.
   - Raman (LA) is pending.
6. **Not computed:** performance relative to district partisanship (Cook PVI and 2024 margins not retrieved); FEC and outside-spending totals; volunteer counts outside NYC [D §f].

**Best-supported explanation:** a combination of five factors:
- a deliberate safe-seat strategy;
- weak or scandal-hit incumbents and open seats;
- an anti-establishment mood among Democratic primary voters (NBC called it "a dangerous political environment for Democratic incumbents");
- coalition field organizing (DSA, WFP, Justice Democrats, Mamdani);
- Israel-aid positioning that appealed to primary electorates.

**Socialism attitudes over the cycle:**
- Gallup, Aug 2026: 43% positive, a record. The rise came from independents. Among ages 18–34, socialism (57%) beats capitalism [F-33].
- NBC, Sept 2026: about 21% positive among registered voters; DSA rated *below* "socialism" [F-34].
- About a third of Democrats identify as democratic socialists (CNN/SSRS) [F-38].
- 50% would vote for a democratic socialist, 41% would not (Economist/YouGov, Oct) [F-39].

**Distinctions the data support:**
1. **Specific policies outpoll the word.** Restoring ACA subsidies, Medicare for All in some wordings, taxing the rich, and NYC public grocery stores poll at or above majority support, while "socialism" stays net-negative among actual voters [F-40, F-23].
2. **Opposition to Trump is the clearest stated motive.** 43% voted against him, versus 16% for.
3. **Candidate support is not the same as ideological support.** DSA House winners ran in seats where any Democratic nominee would win the general.

**What the data cannot establish** [F Part 4]:
- Individual causal motive.
- Any ecological inference, from precinct or district results, about why individuals voted.
- A mandate for any specific program.
- Final subgroup figures, such as Latino voters 62% Democratic or young men +33 Democratic. These will move after reweighting and validated-voter studies.

## 7. Effects on the American people (Q7)

Labels: **PROMISE**, **BILL**, **ENACTED**, **OBSERVED**, **FORECAST**. Officials elected Nov 3 take office in January 2027, so **all federal effects below are prospective.**

### Federal agenda

House Democrats' plan, from ABC, NBC and PBS reporting [F-46]:
- **PROMISE/BILL:** early votes to restore the expired enhanced ACA tax credits, reverse 2025 Medicaid cuts, end the global tariffs and restore congressional trade power, and oppose the Iran war.
- **PROMISE/BILL:** five affordability working groups: housing, health, groceries, gas/utilities and caregiving.
- **PROMISE:** corruption oversight. Jeffries says impeachment is neither ruled in nor ruled out.
- **Schumer:** "legislative razor wire"; investigations [A-27].

### Constraints [F-47]

- **Veto override:** needs about 13 Republican senators at 54 Democratic seats, and about 290 House votes.
- **Filibuster:** cloture needs about 6 Republican senators at 54 Democratic seats.
- **Reconciliation:** could carry ACA subsidies by simple majority, but the bill would still face a veto.
- **Tariffs:** repealing tariff authority requires a statute, which faces a veto.
- **Leverage Democrats do gain:** appropriations, confirmations (including a near "lockout" of Supreme Court nominees [A-27]), subpoenas, and vetoable war-powers resolutions.

**Who would be affected (FORECAST):**
- ACA marketplace enrollees who lost the enhanced subsidies.
- Medicaid enrollees subject to the 2025 cuts.
- Importers and consumers paying tariffs.

No CBO scores were found.

## 8. The Democratic coalition and governing institutions (Q8)

**Margin arithmetic** [E-23]. These are inferences on preliminary seat counts.
- **At 229 Democratic seats**, a party-line bill can lose 11 Democrats. The DSA-connected bloc of **8 cannot defeat it alone**. It would need at least 4 more Democratic defectors.
- **At NBC's projected 258**, Democrats could lose 40 votes. **A larger majority means less DSA leverage.**
- **Speaker vote.** At 229 or more, the 8 cannot deny Jeffries the gavel. A combined holdout of 12 or more progressives could. Wider "uncommitted" sentiment among progressives has been reported, but no organized holdout has been.

## 9. State and local results complicate the national story (Q9)

### Washington State [C]

- **Statewide.** The same electorate **kept the millionaires tax** (repeal failing 41.9 / 58.1), **passed the girls'-sports sex-verification measure** (56.8% yes) and **rejected the Parents' Bill of Rights reenactment** (47.3% yes). KUOW framed this as "socially conservative, fiscally liberal?" Roughly 13–16% of voters must have voted both to keep the tax and for I-638 [C-8].
- **County splits.**
  - King County: 66.5% No on repeal.
  - **Spokane County kept the tax (54.7% No) but backed I-001 (~54% Yes, single source)**, the opposite of the statewide result on I-001.
  - Eight rural eastern counties voted to repeal the tax; Whitman and Walla Walla voted to keep it [C-9–C-11].
- **26th Legislative District** (Gig Harbor / south Kitsap):
  - Election-night Democratic leads in all three seats: Senate Krishnadasan 58.65%, partial snapshot; House Pos. 1 Richards ~56.5%; House Pos. 2 (open) Hernandez Greenfield ~54.8%.
  - Republicans had **topped the August primary** in both House seats [C-12–C-16].
  - History: in 2022 the district split its ticket by margins of 1,276 and 735 votes; in 2024 it split again; the 2025 Senate special cost about $4M [C-17–C-21].
  - Data issues: the Senate snapshot is not comparable with the House totals, and the name of the retiring Pos. 2 Republican ("Valdez" vs. Caldier) is unresolved [C, Conflicts].
- **Intra-Democratic contests.** Both House Democrats who voted against the income tax (Walen, LD48; Morgan, LD29) trailed challengers from their left. In LD32, the Senate candidate who wants to extend the tax to $250K led by 352 votes [C-27, C-28]. **None of these challengers is documented as DSA-connected.**
- **Seattle.** Voters approved a sales-tax increase for transit, a regressive tax, by 72.6% [C-37].
- **DSA in Washington.** The only documented tie is Shaun Scott (LD43), a "former" Seattle DSA member who self-identifies as a socialist. He was re-elected unopposed [C-30, C-43].

### Elsewhere [B §2g]

- **North Carolina** flipped its House to Democrats *and* approved voter ID and an income-tax cap.
- **Colorado** rejected mandatory ICE cooperation *and* approved tougher fentanyl penalties.
- **California** voters are rejecting a billionaire tax in a state electing Democrats statewide.
- **Missouri** protected abortion rights, defeating a ballot item that bundled repeal with a youth gender-care ban.

**Implication.** Within single electorates, results cut across party lines on taxes, social issues, immigration enforcement and crime. **No single ideological label, "socialist" or otherwise, describes these electorates.** The strongest common thread is economic: Washington kept its tax on high earners, while California is rejecting a wealth tax that drew over $304M in opposition spending. ## 11. Final conclusions

Each conclusion is assessed separately on the evidence as of Nov 4, 2026.

### (a) Rejection of Trump and Republican governance: strongly supported, with caveats

**Supporting evidence:**
- Democrats won both chambers.
- They flipped 7 or more Senate seats in Trump-won states (TX, OH, IA, ME, NC, KS, SC) and 5 governorships, all in Trump-won states.
- They flipped about 9 or more legislative chambers, with no reported Republican chamber flips.
- Presidential approval among voters was 38%.
- 43% said they voted to oppose Trump, versus 16% who voted to support him.
- Voters reported negative views of tariffs (65% said they hurt) and of the Iran war (about 6 in 10 said "wrong decision").

**Caveats:**
- Midterm losses for the president's party are the historical norm, so the claim is about **magnitude**. On magnitude it is supported in the Senate, and in the House is in the range of past waves.
- Republicans held Florida (Senate and governor), Louisiana, Nebraska and the safe red states.
- Voters still preferred Republicans on immigration.
- Conservative ballot measures passed in several states that elected Democrats.

The evidence supports a **rejection of the current administration's performance**, especially on cost of living, tariffs, the Iran war and corruption. It **does not establish a rejection of conservative positions across the board.**

### (b) Support for specific Democratic policies: partially supported, issue by issue

**Supported:**
- Voters preferred Democrats on health care (50–30) and the economy (43–37).
- Majorities favor restoring ACA subsidies in pre-election polling.
- Abortion-rights measures won in MO, VA and NV.
- Washington kept its tax on high earners.
- Colorado rejected an ICE-cooperation mandate.

**Contradicted or mixed:**
- California is rejecting a billionaire tax.
- Tough-on-crime measures, voter-ID measures (NC) and citizen-only voting language passed widely.
- Washington passed a restriction on transgender girls in girls' sports.
- Republicans led on immigration.
- NBC: voters were "no more progressive" on issues than in 2024.

**Uncertain:** the House Democratic agenda has not yet been voted on, and survey items on many specific policies were not retrieved. Support for specific policies is **real but selective**. The election is not a mandate for the full Democratic platform.

### (c) Support for democratic socialism: not supported as a national verdict; supported only within limited electorates

**Contradicting evidence:**
- Among 2026 voters, socialism was 33% favorable / 54% unfavorable, and NBC rated DSA *below* "socialism."
- DSA's House wins came almost entirely in **safe Democratic seats where the decisive vote was a primary**, so general-election margins of 78–94% measure partisanship, not socialism.
- The DSA candidates who faced broader or redder electorates lost: Nixon in Florida, Jewett in LA-1, and Vang trailing in CA-7.
- The most prominent left-populist statewide winner, El-Sayed, rejects the socialist label.

**Supporting evidence, within limits:**
- Democratic **primary** electorates in NYC, Denver, Detroit, Philadelphia and East LA chose DSA-endorsed candidates over incumbents or establishment rivals.
- **General electorates** elected DSA-connected mayors in D.C. and Providence by about 81–84%.
- Gonzales-Torres won a November Democrat-vs-Democrat contest, though she ran against an incumbent under ethics review.
- National attitudes are shifting: Gallup records socialism's highest-ever rating (43%), under-35s prefer socialism to capitalism, and about a third of Democrats self-identify as democratic socialists.

**What the data cannot show:** whether individual voters chose these candidates *because of* socialism, rather than because of incumbent weakness, anti-establishment mood, affordability messages, Israel/Gaza or anti-Trump sentiment. Those motives cannot be separated with the available data.

### (d) Increased DSA organizational or legislative influence

**Organizational influence: supported**, though largely self-reported.
- The House delegation goes from 2 to 8.
- New DSA-member mayors in D.C. and Providence join NYC's Mamdani; Los Angeles may follow.
- DSA reports a record number of endorsements and a record legislative delegation in New York.
- Self-reported membership rose from about 50,700 (Oct 2024) to over 120,000 (July 2026).
- The NYC field operation is large, durable and city-linked.
- DSA has shown it can unseat Democratic incumbents in safe seats (4 in 2026).

**Legislative influence: limited, and mostly prospective.**
- Eight members out of 229–268 Democrats **cannot block party-line legislation or the Speaker vote alone**, and a larger majority further reduces their leverage.
- No formal socialist caucus or binding coordination is documented.
- DSA has no senators.
- The incoming members take office in January 2027, so they have no governing record yet.
- Mamdani's record shows influence over what the mayor's office controls directly (the rent freeze) but not over state-level taxation (the millionaire tax).

**Where influence is most plausible (inference):**
- through the 101-member Progressive Caucus;
- through credible primary threats against safe-seat Democrats;
- on cross-pressured votes such as Israel/Gaza;
- in city governments with DSA mayors.

**Bottom line.** The 2026 elections were a broad **anti-incumbent, anti-administration wave** that DSA did not create but used. DSA's own gains came mainly **inside Democratic primaries in safe Democratic seats and cities**, powered by a coalition and field infrastructure built around Mamdani's 2025 campaign. That made DSA more visible and better organized than at any point in its history. It **did not** make democratic socialism the electorate's verdict, and it does **not yet** make DSA a pivotal legislative bloc. Whether its influence becomes durable depends on events after January 2027: caucus formation, Speaker and committee fights, primary challenges in 2028, and the measured results of programs like NYC's rent freeze and childcare expansion.
